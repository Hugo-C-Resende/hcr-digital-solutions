How to read the result

  1. Code letter: comes from the lot size and the inspection level (lot of 1000, level II: letter J).
  2. Sample: how many units you draw at random from the lot. With letter J that is 80 units.
  3. Ac and Re: with AQL 2.5 and 80 units, you accept the lot with up to 5 defects (Ac) and reject it with 6 or more (Re). Each class has its own AQL and its own numbers.
  4. Arrows: for some combinations the table has no plan at that letter and points to another letter's plan, with its sample size. The calculator tells you when this happens (for example, lot of 60, level II, AQL 2.5: letter E, but the plan of letter F is used, with 20 units and 1/2).
  5. 100%: if the plan's sample size is equal to or larger than the lot, inspect every unit.

If you inspect all classes on the same sample, use the largest one and apply each class's criteria: this is a practical convention; confirm the procedure with your customer.

Frequently asked questions

What is AQL?

AQL (Acceptable Quality Limit) is the defect level the buyer accepts, on average, over a continuing series of lots. It sets how many units to inspect and how many defects to accept in the sample. AQL 2.5 does not mean that the lot has 2.5% defects.

Which inspection level should I choose: I, II or III?

General level II is the most common and the starting point when the contract says nothing else. Level I uses smaller samples (less cost, less discrimination) and level III larger ones (stricter). The level should be in the plan agreed with your customer.

What do Ac and Re mean?

Ac is the acceptance number: up to that many defects, the lot passes for that class. Re is the rejection number: from that number on, the lot fails. Example: lot of 3500 units, level II, AQL 2.5: letter L, sample of 200, Ac 10 / Re 11.

What if the lot is small?

With small lots the table often uses an arrow. Lot of 20 units, level II, AQL 2.5: letter C, sample of 5, Ac 0 / Re 1. Lot of 60: letter E, but the arrow points to the plan of letter F, with 20 units and Ac 1 / Re 2. If the sample is equal to or larger than the lot, inspect everything.

Do I follow the standard or my customer's plan?

The plan agreed with your customer comes first: inspection level, AQL per class and how to handle critical defects. The standard (ISO 2859-1 or ANSI/ASQ Z1.4) is the reference for the tables. This calculator helps you read the commonly used tables, but it replaces neither.
