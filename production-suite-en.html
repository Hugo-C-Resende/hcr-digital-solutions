The problem: quoting and production don't talk

In a metal fabrication shop or a furniture workshop, quotes are often built in a spreadsheet, with reference values and a safety margin. Then the job goes into production, material is cut, hours go by, and at the end it is hard to tell whether that job made the profit the quote expected.

Without linking what was quoted to what was really spent on material, waste and hours, every new quote repeats the same guesses.

What we are designing

None of this is ready. This is the list of what we are designing, and it is where pilot companies help us get it right.

Full cost

We are designing a quote that includes material, waste, labour, machines and overheads, not just the cost of material.

Cutting optimisation

We are designing how to make the most of bars and sheets: how to cut to waste less, and how to bring that waste into the quote.

Parametric furniture modules

We are designing modules that adapt to the requested dimensions, to quote and build furniture without redoing everything each time.

Work orders

We are designing the work order with time and progress recording and quality records, linked to the quote it came from.

Quoted vs actual

We are designing the comparison between what was quoted and what was really spent, for each job.

Production without prices

We are designing role-based access, so production sees what it has to make without seeing prices or margins.
